AI that helps you code:
real help or false promise?

More and more developers use artificial intelligence that writes code for them. I wanted to find out whether it really saves time, and whether it causes problems.

What is technology watch?

Doing technology watch means regularly following the news on a topic to stay up to date. In IT, everything changes fast: a developer needs to know what is coming in their job. It is also a skill required in the BTS SIO.

My question

“Does AI really save developers time, without making their work less reliable?”

Three words to follow along.

Generative AI

Artificial intelligence that creates text, images or code when you ask it something. Example: ChatGPT.

Coding assistant

An AI built into the developer's tool that suggests code while they type. Example: GitHub Copilot.

Personal data

Any information about a person (name, e-mail…). In Europe, it is protected by law: the GDPR.

AI in code, in a few dates.

2021

GitHub Copilot

The first mainstream coding assistant: it suggests lines of code as you type.

2022

ChatGPT

At the end of November, anyone can chat with an AI… and ask it for code.

2025

AI agents

AI no longer just suggests: it edits several files and runs tests on its own.

2026

An everyday tool

About two developers in three use it. The question becomes: how to use it well?

It feels faster, but not always

Experienced developers expected AI to save them 24% of their time. In reality, they took 19% longer (METR study).

4 in 10

The code works, but is not always safe

About 4 times in 10, code written by AI contains a security flaw, even when it works (Veracode).

Benefits and risks.

What AI brings

  • Saving time on repetitive tasks
  • Understanding unfamiliar code by asking for an explanation
  • Finding the cause of a bug faster
  • Learning a new technology with examples

What to watch out for

  • Security flaws in the suggested code
  • Wrong but very convincing answers
  • The temptation to stop learning the basics
  • Private data sent to an external service

My watch tools.

Free tools that bring the information to me instead of me having to look for it.

Google Alerts

I get an e-mail as soon as an article mentions my keywords: “IA développement”, “coding assistant”, “AI code security”.

Feedly

An RSS reader: it gathers the latest articles from the sites I follow in one place.

Tech press

Tech news sites such as InfoQ, SD Times or Le Monde Informatique, for news and studies.

LinkedIn

I follow developers and security experts who share and comment on what is new.

Official websites

The CNIL and ANSSI (French authorities), for the rules to follow and security advice.

Major surveys

Reports published every year, such as the Stack Overflow survey or the DORA report, for reliable figures.

How often?

A simple routine, so the watch stays up to date without spending hours on it.

01

Every week

15 minutes

02

1 hour

03

Every year

on release

I read the major surveys as soon as they come out to refresh the key figures.

My takeaways

AI is a good tool, not an autopilot.

01

Always review

Code suggested by AI must be checked and tested before it is used.

02

Don't take it on trust

Feeling faster does not mean being faster.

03

Learn the basics

Without solid basics, you cannot tell whether what the AI suggests is right.
