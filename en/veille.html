Generative AI in software development:
real gain or illusion?

Coding assistants built on generative AI are now part of many developers' daily work. This watch follows what they really bring, what they may degrade (quality, security) and the framework in which to use them. It is directly tied to my future job.

What is technology watch?

Definition

Technology watch means monitoring a field's developments in an organised and regular way: new tools, studies, rules. The goal: stay up to date and make better decisions. It is not reading the news at random, but following a method: a topic, chosen sources, sorting, then a summary.

Why in the BTS SIO?

The framework asks students to organise their professional development, and technology watch is part of it: a developer has to keep up throughout their career.

Why this topic?

Coding assistants are already changing how software is written. As a future developer, I need to know when they really help and when they create risks.

My watch question

“Do coding assistants make developers more efficient without degrading code quality and security?”

Key terms.

Generative AI

Artificial intelligence that can produce content (text, images, code) from a written request, called a “prompt”.

LLM

Large Language Model: the engine of generative AI, trained on huge amounts of text and code.

Coding assistant

A tool built into the editor that suggests code as you type, or answers questions. Example: GitHub Copilot.

AI agent

An AI that does more than suggest: it chains actions on its own (editing several files, running tests) to reach a goal.

My method, in four steps.

01

Target

A precise question and keywords in French and English: “assistant de code”, “AI-generated code security”, “developer productivity”.

02

Collect

Google Alerts on the keywords, RSS feeds from the tech press, annual reports (Stack Overflow, DORA), official sites such as the CNIL.

03

Check

Who publishes? When? How was the figure obtained? I go back to the original source and note each study's limits.

04

Summarise

I sort the information into three themes, keep only sourced and dated figures, and update this page.

Three themes to see clearly.

Theme 01

Productivity gains: perceived or measured?

The DORA 2025 report (nearly 5,000 professionals) says more than 80% believe AI has increased their productivity. Yet the METR study on real tasks by experienced developers measured the opposite: 19% more time, while they expected to save 24%. The study dates from 2025 and covers the tools of the time: the gain depends on context, experience and tools.

Theme 02

Quality and security of generated code

According to Veracode's summer 2026 leaderboard (11 models; 100+ tracked since the programme began), models pass 56% of security tests on average, almost unchanged from a year ago: Python reaches 63%, Java only 30%. These tests cover raw models, with no human review or guardrails. DORA describes AI as an amplifier: it strengthens an organisation's strengths but also its weaknesses, and delivery instability keeps rising.

Theme 03

Responsible use and legal framework

In the 2026 Stack Overflow survey, 78.7% say source attribution matters and only 24% say their employer has approved tools or an official policy. On the legal side, the CNIL published its recommendations in July 2025 for developing GDPR-compliant AI systems, noting that the GDPR applies in many cases to models trained on personal data. A habit worth keeping: never paste API keys or personal data into an assistant.

My takeaways

AI speeds up writing, not responsibility.

01

Review, test, understand

Generated code is checked before it is shipped, especially for security.

02

Measure rather than believe

Perceived productivity is no substitute for measurement.

03

Keep the fundamentals

For a student, the assistant must not replace learning: without the fundamentals, it is impossible to judge what it suggests.

My sources, and what they are worth.

Not all sources are equal. For each one, I give its type, why it is useful and its limits. Figures are quoted as published.

Survey6 Oct 2026

Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026 ↗

The big annual developer survey: AI use, trust, company rules.

Strength
Very large: more than 30,000 responses from 169 countries.
Limit
Self-reported: it measures what developers say, not what they do.

Read via : IT Brief

Scientific studyJul 2025

METR productivity study ↗

A controlled experiment: experienced developers carry out real tasks, with or without AI, and the time is measured.

Strength
Actual measurement, not a feeling: that is what makes it valuable.
Limit
Only 16 developers, and early-2025 tools that have evolved since.

Read via : TechCrunch

Company report28 Jul 2026

Veracode, security of AI-generated code ↗

Repeated security tests on many AI models, tracked over time.

Strength
Same method from one year to the next: results can be compared.
Limit
Veracode sells security tools, and the models are tested without human review.

Read via : SD Times

Research report29 Sep 2025

DORA, State of AI-assisted Software Development ↗

DORA, a research programme backed by Google Cloud, studies the performance of software teams.

Strength
Nearly 5,000 professionals surveyed, a recognised reference in the industry.
Limit
Productivity is mostly self-reported, and Google builds AI tools itself.

Read via : InfoQ

Public authority22 Jul 2025

CNIL, recommendations on AI systems ↗

The French data protection authority's official recommendations for developing AI systems in line with the GDPR.

Strength
Official French source, the reference on data protection.
Limit
It sets a legal framework but does not measure how effective the tools are.
